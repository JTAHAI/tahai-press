The publisher-owned newsroom

Set the record in type.
Keep the record in Git.

TAHAI Press turns articles, images, public records, PDFs, and community reporting into a formal, fast, accessible publication. Launch Desk guides a first-time publisher from an untouched demo to a backed-up launch package in thirteen short steps. Its browser-only Contributor Composer gives nontechnical writers a clean first-draft workflow with local saved copies and JSON handoff. Media Desk crops, compresses, describes, credits, and exports publication-ready images without an upload service. Professional Desk adds clear classifications, section fronts, continuing series, correction history, trust notes, related coverage, and permanent citations. Operational Polish adds private newsroom health, media inventory, performance budgets, and CMS-managed puzzles. Reader Reach adds installable offline reading, private saved stories, accessible sharing, and a printable current edition without adding a reader account or public backend.

Launch Desk · Version 1.8

From untouched demo to first story in thirteen clear steps.

Launch Desk is the first-day newsroom for people who should not need to understand JSON, deployment terminology, or theme systems before they can publish. One primary action appears on each screen. Safe defaults fill ordinary decisions, progress stays visible, the publication preview updates before anything is applied, and the desk explains unfamiliar fields in plain language.

Undo history and backups remain local. The final package contains ordinary TAHAI Press files, and Git still provides review and recovery. No setup account, analytics service, database, or public attribution requirement is added.

Contributor Composer · Current edition

A fast desk for the first draft—and the next one.

Quick Story keeps the writing surface short: headline, summary, article, contributor, category, and an optional image. The browser generates the slug, estimates reading time, previews the newspaper layout, flags common accessibility problems, keeps up to 20 local draft copies, reopens TAHAI Press JSON, and exports an ordinary contributor package.

The draft remains in local browser storage. No text or selected image is uploaded to TAHAI Press, an AI service, analytics provider, or application database.

Media Desk · Version 2.2

Prepare the image and preserve the editorial context.

Media Desk gives a small newsroom the image workflow it actually needs: constrained publishing presets, browser-local cropping, focal-point control, WebP or JPEG compression, a required image description, source credit, rights notes, and a clean repository handoff. The source image never leaves the browser.

The downloaded manifest keeps crop coordinates, output dimensions, accessibility text, credit, rights, and ready-to-paste TAHAI Press article fields together. No hosted media service, database, account, analytics call, or remote image fetch is introduced.

Accessibility Edition · Version 1.4

The reader controls the reading surface.

TAHAI Press treats accessibility as part of publishing, not a compliance footnote. Editors receive plain-language guidance before handoff. The build rejects broken accessibility contracts. Readers can adjust type size, line spacing, measure, surface, link underlines, decoration, and motion without signing in or sending preferences to a server.

Operational Polish · Version 1.6

Keep the newsroom healthy without building another system.

Operational Polish turns routine maintenance into readable, private build artifacts. TAHAI Press inventories media, finds missing and unused uploads, detects exact duplicates, enforces explicit performance budgets, and summarizes the newsroom state before deployment.

The reports remain in local and GitHub Actions artifacts. They are never copied into the public site, and they require no monitoring vendor, analytics account, application database, or administration server.

Reader Reach · Version 1.7

Bring readers back without creating another account system.

Reader Reach gives a static publication the return-visit conveniences readers expect while keeping the privacy and operational simplicity of ordinary files. The latest edition can be installed and read offline, articles can be saved on one device, links can be shared through the browser, and a complete current edition can be printed like a newspaper.

Saved lists and preferences remain in local browser storage. The service worker caches only same-origin publication pages and assets. No reader profile, analytics account, notification vendor, hosted database, or social-sharing script is introduced.

The production line

Simple enough to explain on one page.

Editors work in Pages CMS. Content and media are committed to GitHub. TAHAI Press validates the record and builds plain static files. Cloudflare Pages publishes them globally. There is no application database to patch, no WordPress runtime to babysit, and no proprietary export standing between the publisher and the archive.
